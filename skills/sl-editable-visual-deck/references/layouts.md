# Layouts

Twelve layouts ship in `references/template.html` (one commented example each). Pick by what the slide has to do; never use the same layout on more than two consecutive slides. Keep every slide under 40 words of body text; put the rest in speaker notes.

| Layout | Use it for | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| `title` | The opening promise of the deck | title under 10 words, subtitle under 22 |
| `agenda` | The parts of the talk | 3-6 items, under 6 words each |
| `statement` | One idea the room must remember | under 18 words + one note |
| `bullets` | A short argument with supporting points | 2-5 bullets, under 14 words each |
| `cards` | 3-4 parallel ideas, options or principles | heading under 4 words, body under 18 |
| `stats` | 2-4 numbers that prove the point | only real, sourced numbers |
| `quote` | A voice from a customer, user or expert | under 35 words, always attributed |
| `timeline` | A sequence, plan or history | 3-5 steps, labels under 3 words |
| `compare` | Before/after, us/them, option A/B | the right side is the recommendation |
| `chart` | One comparison of 3-6 values | the last bar is highlighted, so put the point you are making last; takeaway first |
| `visual` | A breather between dense sections | caption under 25 words |
| `closing` | The decision or next steps | 2-4 actions, each with an owner |

Action titles: every title is a full sentence with a verb ("Weekly check-ins cut churn by a third"), not a label ("Churn"). Read the titles in order; they must tell the story on their own.
